ROOM 310

An Interactive Cinematic Mystery Thriller

Genre: Psychological Mystery / Thriller / Interactive Drama
Format: Third-person / first-person cinematic gameplay
Estimated first playthrough: 4–6 hours
Main endings: 10
Setting: A luxury hotel in a modern coastal city
Time period: Present day
Core themes: Truth, guilt, manipulation, memory, surveillance, corruption, revenge

1. THE PREMISE

At 2:13 AM, Daniel Vale, a 31-year-old investigative journalist, receives a phone call from an unknown number.

The caller is a woman.

She sounds frightened.

"If you're hearing this, I'm already dead."

Daniel asks who she is.

She answers:

"Room 310."

The call ends.

Minutes later, Daniel receives a hotel reservation confirmation under his name.

The Halcyon Hotel — Room 310.

Daniel never made the reservation.

He goes anyway.

Inside Room 310, he discovers blood, a broken phone, a hidden camera, and a photograph of himself taken three days earlier.

The woman who called him is Claire Mercer, a former hotel employee who supposedly died eight months ago.

Except someone has been using her phone tonight.

And someone inside the hotel knows Daniel has arrived.

10. HOW THE BRANCHING WORKS

The game should NOT have ten completely separate stories.

Instead, it uses persistent variables.

Example:

type StoryState = {
    trustClaire: number;
    trustMaya: number;
    trustNoah: number;
    trustVictor: number;

    claireAlive: boolean;
    mayaAlive: boolean;
    noahAlive: boolean;
    eliasAlive: boolean;

    discoveredArchive: boolean;
    discoveredRoom310B: boolean;
    discoveredOrpheus: boolean;
    discoveredCircle: boolean;

    liedToMaya: boolean;
    stoleNoahKey: boolean;
    destroyedEvidence: boolean;

    playerMemoryRecovered: boolean;
};


Every choice modifies the state.

Example:

choose("trust_maya");

story.trustMaya += 20;
story.trustClaire -= 5;

if (story.trustMaya > 60) {
    unlock("federal_route");
}


This means a tiny decision in Chapter 1 can matter four hours later.

15. CORE GAMEPLAY LOOP

The game should constantly alternate between:

EXPLORE → OBSERVE → INVESTIGATE → CHOOSE → CONSEQUENCE

Not:

WALK → CUTSCENE → CHOOSE → CUTSCENE

The player should physically investigate scenes.

For example:

Daniel can:

Open drawers.

Inspect fingerprints.

Read documents.

Listen to recordings.

Examine photographs.

Search computers.

Observe NPC behavior.

Follow suspicious characters.

Hide.

Eavesdrop.

Lie.

Tell the truth.

Manipulate people.

Decide who receives evidence.

This makes the game feel like an actual game rather than an interactive movie.

16. THE MOST IMPORTANT DESIGN RULE

Never tell the player:

"This choice will affect the story."

Instead:

Let the player forget what they chose.

Then, 45 minutes later:

Show the consequence.

For example:

Early:

Daniel lies to Maya.

Later:

Maya asks him the same question.

Daniel gives a different answer.

Maya notices.

She doesn't say anything.

Three chapters later, she refuses to trust Daniel.

The player realizes:

"Oh shit. This happened because of what I said earlier."

That's the feeling the game should constantly create.

The mystery has three layers.

Layer 1 — What happened to Claire?

Claire supposedly died.

But she's alive.

Why?

Layer 2 — What happened to Elias?

Daniel's brother supposedly died.

But he's alive.

Why was his death faked?

Layer 3 — What is Room 310?

The answer:

Room 310 isn't important because of what happened inside it.

It's important because everyone involved in the story eventually passes through it.

It's the location where The Circle manipulated people, stored evidence, erased memories, and created false identities.

By the end, the player realizes:

The entire game was built around one question:

Who controls the truth?

And the final answer depends on the player's choices.
